How to Separate Business and Personal Money in Nigeria (and Pay Yourself a Salary)

Mixing shop money with personal money hides losses. Simple rules for owner's drawings that work with cash, transfer and POS.

When your money and the shop's money are mixed, you cannot tell whether the business profits, and records you show others are hard to trust.

  • Use a separate account or sub-account for the business if your bank or wallet provider allows it. If not, a separate wallet or a labelled envelope for cash is a start.
  • Receive business transfers into the business account only.
  • Pay business costs from the business account or shop cash.
  • Pay yourself a set amount on a set day. Write it in your notebook as "owner's drawings".
  • Do not borrow from the till quietly. If you take money, write it down the same day.
  • Pay personal spending from your personal account.

Fictional example: Net profit is ₦54,000. If you take ₦70,000 during the month, the business loses ₦16,000 of its own money and will eventually run out of stock money.

If you put your own money into the business, note it as "owner money in".

Quick tips

If the shop's money and your money are mixed, you can't tell whether the business is making a profit. Pay yourself a fixed amount on a fixed day. If the business made ₦54,000 profit but you took ₦70,000, the shop just lost ₦16,000 of its stock money. Even a labelled envelope for shop cash is a start.
