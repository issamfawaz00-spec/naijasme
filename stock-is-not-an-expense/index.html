How to Record Stock and Expenses Correctly in a Small Business (Don't Count Stock Twice)

Goods you buy to resell become a cost only when sold. How to record expenses, count stock at month end and stop small costs eating your profit.

Expenses

In Supplier, note who you paid, for example "Fuel seller". There is no description column.

Typical expenses: rent, transport and delivery, generator fuel, data, wages, packaging, POS and bank charges, repairs and levies.

Keep receipts, or write one line in your notebook the same day. An expense of ₦300 a day is ₦9,000 in a 30-day month, and forgotten expenses make profit look bigger than it is.

Stock: do not double count

Many users leave it out and compare the estimate with real balances.

Stock tab

Fictional example: You open with 20 cartons of a noodle brand, buy 30 and sell 38. Closing is 20 + 30 − 38 = 12 cartons. With a reorder level of 15, the flag shows REORDER.

If they differ, something was sold but not recorded, stolen, damaged or given away. Find out why. Do not just change the numbers.

Quick tips

Common record-keeping mistake: writing the goods you buy to resell as an "expense" AND counting their cost when you sell them. Your profit then looks too low. Goods for resale become a cost when they are sold. Rent, fuel and transport are expenses.
