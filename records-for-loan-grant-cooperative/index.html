Business Records for a Loan, Grant or Cooperative Application in Nigeria

What lenders, grant programmes and cooperatives may ask to see, and how to prepare honest monthly sales, expense and profit records.

Lenders, grant bodies and cooperatives set their own requirements. Always ask what they want and follow their instructions. Nothing here guarantees any loan, grant or approval.

Organised records may help you prepare what you are asked for. They may ask to see:

  • Sales records: how much you sell each month and how it is paid for.
  • Expense records: what it costs to run the business.
  • Profit: a simple profit and loss for recent months.
  • Cash position: money in and out, and what you hold now.
  • Stock: roughly what goods you hold.
  • Debts: who owes you and who you owe.
  • Your purpose: what you want the money for and how you will use it.

Check it against your real cash, bank and POS balances before you show it to anyone, and explain or correct any gap. Bank or wallet statements show the real position.

How to prepare from the tracker

  1. Keep each month in its own clearly named file, such as "MyShop_Tracker_March".
  2. Keep a folder of the PDFs, one per month, plus your business bank or wallet statements.
  3. Write a short note (half a page): what you sell, who your customers are, your location and how long you have traded.
  4. Write what you need the money for, with a rough cost list, such as "more stock: ₦X, a shelf: ₦Y".
  5. Be honest. Do not change figures to look better. Lenders may compare your records with your statements.

If a cooperative asks for savings records or business information, a monthly summary and separated business money show you are organised. Ask what form they need. The more months of records you have, the more you have to show. Start now, even if the business is small.
