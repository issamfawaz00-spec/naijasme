How to Calculate Profit in a Small Business in Nigeria: Gross Profit vs Net Profit

Step-by-step Naira example: sales, cost of goods, gross profit, rent, fuel, POS charges and net profit - and why a busy shop can still lose money.

Learn them well.

  • Gross Profit = Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold.
  • Gross Margin % = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue. It shows how many kobo of each naira of sales is left after paying for the goods.
  • Operating expenses by category, then Total Expenses. A check line shows any amount not in a listed category. It should be 0.
  • Net Profit = Gross Profit − Total Expenses, and Net Margin %.

Worked example (fictional)

ItemAmount
Revenue (all sales)₦480,000
Cost of Goods Sold₦336,000
Gross Profit (480,000 − 336,000)₦144,000
Rent₦40,000
Generator fuel and electricity₦20,000
Transport₦15,000
Wages (part-time helper)₦10,000
Phone data and POS charges₦5,000
Total Expenses₦90,000
Net Profit (144,000 − 90,000)₦54,000

Gross Margin = 144,000 ÷ 480,000 = 0.30, or 30 kobo in every naira of sales. These numbers are invented to teach the method. They do not describe any real or typical shop.

How to read it

  • Gross profit is not what you take home. In the example only ₦54,000 is left of ₦144,000.
  • Net profit is not cash in your pocket. Stock bought for next month, money customers owe you and personal withdrawals all affect cash.
  • Watch the margin month to month. If it drops, suppliers may have raised prices, you may be discounting too much, or goods may be going missing.
  • A busy shop can still lose money. High sales with low margin and high expenses can leave a small or negative net profit.
