Credit sales: why business is moving but your pocket is empty

Debtors, creditors and good habits for selling on credit without running out of cash.

  • Debtors owe you, such as customers who took goods on credit.
  • Creditors are people you owe, such as suppliers who gave you goods before you paid.

Good habits for debtors

  • Record the credit sale with the customer's name and agree a payment date at the time.
  • Keep credit for people you know. Set a limit per customer.
  • A credit sale is revenue, but it is not cash yet. Profit may look good while your pocket is empty.

Good habits for creditors

  • List every supplier you owe, with amount and date.
  • Pay on time where you can.
  • Before you spend on something new, check how much you owe suppliers.

Fictional example: A customer takes goods worth ₦12,000 on credit and pays ₦5,000 two weeks later. Balance = ₦7,000. You count the full ₦12,000 as a sale, but only ₦5,000 has arrived as cash.

Quick tips

"Business is moving but my pocket is empty." Check your credit sales. A customer takes ₦12,000 of goods and pays ₦5,000 two weeks later. Your sales went up by ₦12,000, but only ₦5,000 reached you. Write every credit sale down with a name and a payment date, and check the list every Friday.
